The Story Behind Direct AI Automation

For over two decades, Daniel Rodriguez worked at the intersection of cybersecurity and crisis response — protecting federal agencies and Fortune 500 enterprises from some of the most sophisticated threats in the world. His work spanned CISA's Hunt and Incident Response Team, the Pentagon Computer Incident Response Team, the DoD Cyber Crime Center, the U.S. Department of State CIRT, and the NCUA — alongside corporate roles at Synchrony Financial, Biogen, and E*Trade Financial.

In that work, he authored incident response SOPs, playbooks, and detection content deployed across agency-level and enterprise SOCs. He understands what it means to build systems that handle sensitive data responsibly — and what happens when they don't.

Throughout those years, one thing separated the agencies and enterprises that recovered quickly from incidents and those that didn't: documented, repeatable processes. Daniel authored the standard operating procedures, response playbooks, and detection workflows that teams relied on when seconds counted. That same discipline — building systems that are precise, reliable, and leave nothing to chance — is the foundation of every automation delivered at Direct AI Automation. When we set up your lead capture, follow-up sequences, and booking system, it's not improvised. It's engineered to eliminate wasted time, reduce operational costs, and create the kind of consistent, repeatable business processes that free you to focus on growth — not admin.

After a deliberate sabbatical focused on AI, large language models, and emerging technology, Daniel returned with a clear mission: bring the same security-first, systematic discipline from federal incident response to the AI automation space — and make it accessible to the small businesses that need it most.

Direct AI Automation was built for NYC small businesses — the dental offices, law firms, salons, and home service companies that are losing customers every day to missed calls and slow follow-ups, but don't have the enterprise resources to fix it. That gap is exactly what we close.
